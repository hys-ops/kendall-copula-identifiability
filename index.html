Kendall distributions identify Archimedean copulas in every finite dimension

For each fixed dimension d ≥ 2, the population distribution of C(U), where U has distribution function C, uniquely determines the Archimedean copula C.

Read the paper · 5-page PDFReproduce the proof · ZIP
Main theorem · fixed d ≥ 2

Law of C₁(U₁) = law of C₂(U₂)
⇒ C₁ = C₂ on [0, 1]d

Their generators satisfy ψ₂(r) = ψ₁(cr) for a constant c > 0 and every r ≥ 0. This rescaling leaves the copula unchanged.

Research path · what suggested the proof

From inverse reconstruction to the Kendall proof

  1. Inspiration · no logical dependence

    Recover hidden information from a transformed observation

    An inverse-reconstruction idea from an earlier exploration related to the Riemann hypothesis suggested a question: what can a smoothed observation still reveal about the object behind it? This was a way to choose the question, not a theorem used below.

  2. Transfer and search

    Ask what the Kendall law preserves

    The 2011 conjecture asks whether the law of a scalar score determines the original copula. A low-dimensional result prompted a search for an argument that would extend to all dimensions. The breakthrough was to use the classical Williamson representation and compare the two reciprocal radial quantiles at the same probability rank.

  3. New proof mechanism

    Make disagreement impossible

    The comparison gives a weighted difference identity and a positive weight bound whenever the two normalized values are separated. Together they force a constant ratio of quantiles. Dimension changes only the exponent of the kernel; the argument does not proceed by induction.

See the logical proof dependencies below.

Mathematical dependencies

From the observed law to the copula

The proof uses the same comparison in every dimension. The dimension enters through the exponent m = d − 1. Discrete, singular and mixed radial laws, and generators with finite right endpoints, are included.

  1. 01Classical input

    Represent the generator

    The Williamson representation writes ψ(r) as the expectation of (1 − rT)+m for the reciprocal radius T. The score C(U) is ψ(1/T).

  2. 02Proposition 2

    Read the law at every rank

    The lower Kendall quantile at rank p is the integral of (1 − x(s)/x(p))m over 0 < s < p, where x is the lower quantile of T. Ties contribute zero shortfall. Equal population Kendall laws therefore give equal profiles rank by rank.

  3. 03Equations (4), (5), (8)

    Two facts force rigidity

    Compare z = x/(x + y) for the two reciprocal radial quantiles at the same rank. A difference of powers gives both facts below.

    Common-measure identity

    At every rank p, the weighted differences from z(p) integrate to zero against the same measure dμ(s) = (x(s) + y(s)) ds.

    Gap bound on weights

    Whenever two values of z are separated by a fixed positive gap, the corresponding weight has a positive lower bound.

    Lemma 4 + Theorem 3: Together these force z to be constant, so the two quantiles are proportional.

  4. 04Theorem 1

    Recover the copula on the closed cube

    Proportional radial quantiles rescale the generators. The inverse formula cancels that scale in the open cube; continuity then gives equality on the whole closed cube.

Why the integral identity is decisive. At a rank near a running high, the identity forces almost all the earlier mass to lie near that high. At a later rank near a low, that same mass contributes a positive deviation that the rest cannot cancel. Applying the reasoning to both z and 1 − z rules out either direction of change.

Context

The conjecture and its prerequisites

The bivariate case was known. Genest, Nešlehová and Ziegel proved the trivariate and finitely supported cases and posed the general d ≥ 4 identification question as Conjecture 1. Kasper, Dietrich and Trutschnig restated it as injectivity on normalized Williamson measures. Theorem 1 of the manuscript proves that conjecture.
